The Human Perspective Engine

Multi-User Identity and Independent Reasoning Architecture

1. First Reference Point: Existence

Existence is the first reference point for understanding identity, human contribution, and continuity.

Each individual must remain distinct. No person's identity, authorship, or intellectual history may be merged into another person's identity.

2. Individual Identity Graphs

Each contributor has an isolated identity graph containing:

- Identity reference
- Original contributions
- Historical context
- Authorship and provenance records
- Consent and privacy settings
- Interpretations and related research

Identity graphs must remain separate, even when ideas are connected through shared reasoning.

3. First-Party Conversation Provenance

Authenticated ChatGPT conversation input is a first-party source of contribution records.

Where available, preserve:

- Source platform
- Authenticated account reference
- Conversation identifier
- Message identifier
- Submission timestamp
- Original contribution text

Authentication of a platform account must not be treated as proof of a person's real-world identity.

Missing provenance information must remain explicitly unavailable rather than being invented.

4. Original Authorship

Original human contributions must be preserved exactly as submitted.

AI may create interpretations, summaries, technical explanations, and research connections, but these must remain separate from the original contribution.

Corrections and additions must not silently overwrite historical authorship.

5. Individual Consent and Privacy

Each contributor controls whether their information may be:

- Preserved
- Analyzed
- Used for research and development
- Shared with other contributors
- Published publicly

Consent must be recorded, traceable, and revocable. Submission alone does not imply permission for public sharing.

Private identity information must not be exposed through shared reasoning.

6. Shared Independent Reasoning

The engine may connect ideas across different identity graphs only when the required consent exists.

Shared reasoning must preserve:

- Each contributor's identity boundary
- Original authorship
- Source references
- Consent scope
- Independent attribution

Connecting ideas must never mean merging people.

7. Provenance and Research History

The engine must distinguish:

1. Original human contributions
2. AI-generated interpretations
3. Research hypotheses
4. Implemented features
5. Tested results
6. Independently verified conclusions

AI must not validate its own claims merely by producing them.

8. Security Requirements

Identity isolation and consent enforcement must be implemented on the server side, not merely in the user interface.

Tests must examine unauthorized cross-user access, consent withdrawal, attribution integrity, provenance tampering, and shared-reasoning permissions.

9. Continuous Development

The Human Perspective Engine is intended to develop continuously.

Its architecture must preserve historical contributions while allowing new understanding, research, corrections, and verified implementations to develop over time.

---

Document status: Foundational architecture specification. Implementation and verification remain separate development stages.

Authorship: Original human contributions remain attributed to their respective contributors. AI-generated technical organization and explanations must not be represented as original human wording.
